# 23 — Review Actions

Disposition register for the three independent reviews of the plan set. Every finding is recorded with a severity, a disposition, and where it was applied. **Nothing is silently dropped.**

Statuses: `FIXED` (applied to a plan file) · `ACCEPTED` (accepted, folded into a later phase) · `DEFERRED` (deliberately not done now, with reason) · `REJECTED` (the reviewer is wrong, with reason).

---

## How the reviews were run

Three independent reviews, each with a deliberately different lens so the perspectives would not converge:

| Reviewer | Lens | What it found |
|---|---|---|
| Technical / architecture | "What breaks at 3am, who gets paged, and which claims are true?" | 18 blocking defects, 27 correctness risks, 7+ contradictions. **Ran `prisma validate` against the schema** and generated the DDL. |
| Psychometrics / fairness | A measurement scientist: "are these numbers defensible, and is this fair to students?" | 21 methodological errors, 14 unaddressed validity threats, 12 unfairness scenarios. Found a **citation that had been edited to support the opposite of what it says**. |
| Delivery / executability | A delivery lead: "will this actually ship, and is any of this theatre?" | Sizing understated **2.7×** against the plan's own letters; a critical path through a phase the plan forbids starting early; a gate on 220 sims that cannot pass; no deployment task; no third-party accounts; 10 missing risks. |

Two of three initial reviewer invocations returned empty output. Those were re-run; the results below are from the successful runs. **A returned-empty review is a failure, not a clean bill of health, and is recorded as such.**

## A. Technical review — blocking defects

| ID | Finding | Sev | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **B1** | **The Prisma schema did not validate — 10 errors.** `enum AttemptEvent` collided with `model AttemptEvent`; 7 missing back-relations; `Simulation` had **no primary key** (`@@unique([id,version])` is not an identifier, so no `FOR UPDATE`, no RLS, every upsert needs the compound key). `prisma validate` is not a CI gate, which is why this reached the plan. | **Critical** | `FIXED` — schema rewritten; **`prisma validate` + `prisma migrate diff --exit-code` added as blocking CI gates** (`P0-T4`, `17-TESTING.md` §1). The "no schema gate" root cause is the actual finding and it is now a gate. |
| **B2** | `Question.resourceVersionId @unique` permits **one** snapshot per resource version, so a pooled slot needing 12 items is impossible. `INV-BANK-3` unimplementable. Snapshots also carried a non-null `bankId`, so every "how many items in this bank" query double-counted. | **Critical** | `FIXED` — dropped `@unique`, added `@@index`, added `isSnapshot`, added `@@unique([resourceVersionId, snapshotOfId])`. |
| **B3** | **17 audit-critical timestamps were `TIMESTAMP(3)` without time zone** while 91 were `Timestamptz(3)`. Retention sweeps, session revocation and the DSAR completion marker all read them; Postgres silently reinterprets. | **Critical** | `FIXED` — all corrected. **Added a CI gate that greps generated DDL for `TIMESTAMP(3)` without `TZ`** — a one-line gate that would have caught it. |
| **B4** | `User.email` unique **case-sensitively**, while roster re-import, placeholder matching, magic links and invitations all assume case-insensitive matching. | High | `FIXED` — `@db.Citext` + an explicit `emailNormalized` column as the only lookup key, so normalisation is a code invariant and not a collation accident. |
| **B5** | Deleting a classroom **cascaded away every grade, attempt and receipt**, contradicting `INV-CLASSROOM-2` and the verifiable-receipt guarantee. | High | `FIXED` — `Restrict` on the grade-bearing relations; deletion is a 30-day `DELETING` window plus a PII-only purge job. |
| **B6** | **The sim-origin CSP breaks every simulation.** `default-src 'none'` blocked the sim's own stylesheet; `script-src 'self'` **does not match an opaque origin**, so the bundle was blocked; no `img-src`/`font-src`. `Cross-Origin-Resource-Policy: same-origin` also fails for an opaque-origin frame. | **Critical** | `FIXED` — explicit host in `script-src`/`style-src`/`font-src`, `img-src 'self' data:`, `form-action 'none'`, `base-uri 'none'`; CORP moved to the app origin. Also amended the honesty of "no network" in `INV-SIM-1` to "no same-origin reach and no CSP-permitted egress", because CSP does not cover WebRTC/STUN or DNS prefetch. |
| **B7** | `GET /session/:id/sync` was **unauthenticated** (credential = path param) and `AttemptSession.id` was `uuid(7)` — **time-ordered, therefore enumerable**. Anyone could walk a student's exam. | High | `FIXED` — `sync` requires `x-attempt-session`; **bearer-capability ids switched to `uuid(4)`**, with `uuid(7)` retained for foreign keys only. |
| **B8** | The idempotency ledger recorded **rejected** writes, so a retry after a `409 WINDOW_CLOSED` returned a false `2xx SAVED` — the student saw a green tick and the answer was gone. Directly broke the "no acknowledged save lost" promise. | **Critical** | `FIXED` — rejections go to `AttemptEvent(LATE_SAVE_REJECTED)`, never the ledger; the ledger is now purely a success ledger, and replays return the **original** response bytes. |
| **B9** | Per-question timers started on the first **write**, so an untouched question never expired and `LOCK`/`AUTO_SUBMIT` never fired. A reading-time limit was not enforceable. | **Critical** | `FIXED` — an explicit server-side `open` transition; `questionOpenedAt` set by `COALESCE(...)` under a set-once rule; opening question *n* auto-closes *n−1* under one-at-a-time; per-question deadline clamped to the attempt deadline. |
| **B10** | `WINDOW_CLOSING` shed answer writes inside the grace window, **discarding** answers the plan had promised to keep — and `P8-T9` put "reject late writes" and "write shedding" in the same task, so the bug was encoded in the task boundary. | **Critical** | `FIXED` — `WINDOW_CLOSING` removed from the answer path. Shedding is transport-layer `429 + Retry-After` on **telemetry only**. `P8-T9` split; the answer path never sheds. |
| **B11** | `strikeCount Int` **cannot represent five independent thresholds** — a student who left fullscreen 12 times would trip the `tabHides: 3` threshold and be terminated. The mechanism the whole integrity product rests on. | **Critical** | `FIXED` — `AttemptStrikeCounter { attemptId, kind }` with an atomic upsert returning the count, which also fixes lost updates under concurrent batches. `ExamAttempt.strikeCount` deleted. |
| **B12** | "Single active attempt" and "single active session" were enforced only in application code; Prisma cannot express partial unique indexes. Two concurrent `start` calls created two live attempts. | High | `FIXED` — hand-written partial unique indexes in the first migration, `23505` → `409 ATTEMPT_ALREADY_ACTIVE`. The schema header now states that Prisma-expressible constraints are **not** the whole truth. |
| **B13** | QTI export and the xAPI statement route were **key-exfiltration surfaces with no gate, no audit and no warning**, and the xAPI route was specified to return `scored` statements from a student-facing caller. | High | `FIXED` — QTI export is teacher-or-owner only, requires an acknowledgement naming the item count and key exposure, and writes an `AuditEvent` with item ids. xAPI filters on `releasedAt IS NOT NULL` **in SQL**. Both added to the `14` §10 checklist. |
| **B14** | **Sim graders were dynamically imported into the long-lived worker, in-process, with the database pool in scope.** A bad grader bundle could `process.exit()` the worker mid-release, OOM it, or poison the module cache. The sandbox analysis covered only the browser. | High | `FIXED` — graders run in a `node:worker_thread` with a memory cap, wall-clock timeout and frozen globals; the esbuild metafile asserts `grader.js` imports only the SDK and **zero Node builtins**. `ADR-0012` amended to state the isolation boundary. |
| **B15** | `sim-state` was "accepted and flagged" by design with no size cap — an unbounded write amplifier into JSONB on the primary. The manifest's `stateSchema` was `{ type: "object" }`, so validation rejected nothing. | High | `FIXED` — 64 KB per-state cap, per-attempt total budget, host-owned state schema, oversize states to S3, and a stated storage plan. |
| **B16** | **The 5,000-attempt release could not meet its own SLO**, "verify-all then one transaction" is a TOCTOU window, `membership Json` is a 190 KB unqueryable blob, and RPO 15 min could lose an acknowledged release. The plan's most important correctness claim did not hold at the stated scale. | **Critical** | `FIXED` — the design was **replaced**: release becomes a **single-row gate**. `ReleaseBatchMember { batchId, attemptId }`, and student visibility is `EXISTS (… batch status = 'RELEASED')`. Release is one `UPDATE`. `INV-RELEASE-1` becomes trivially true instead of expensively true, and resumable/idempotent for free. `membership Json` deleted. RPO restated: 0 for submissions and receipts. |
| **B17** | The 6-character join code was brute-forceable in about a day, and the alphabet **contradicted its own rationale** — `8` and `B` are both present, `5` and `S` are both present, so two of the three claimed disambiguations were not applied. | High | `FIXED` — 8 characters, alphabet corrected, and codes derived as `HMAC(secret, classroomId ‖ counter)` so regeneration is instant and revocation is a counter bump. |
| **B18** | LTI grade passback is **irreversible** (AGS has no un-post) and the plan never said so. A regrade lowering a score by 10 points is pushed to the student's LMS. | Medium | `FIXED` — irreversibility stated, confirming dialog required when an LTI binding exists, and a recorded decision on whether a regrade pushes a correction or is suppressed for AGS-bound attempts. |

## B. Technical review — correctness risks

| ID | Finding | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | `EXPIRED` attempts could never be released — no path to `GRADED`, so any class with one non-submitter needed a permanent override. | `FIXED` — the sweep transitions `IN_PROGRESS → EXPIRED → grade.auto → GRADED`; `EXPIRED` is non-terminal in the release gate and has a row in the gradebook. |
| C2 | Three documents disagreed on what a cron submit produces. | `FIXED` — one answer: cron submit is `SUBMITTED` with `submittedBy: CRON`; `EXPIRED` is reserved for an assignment window closing with no attempt. |
| C3 | **Inngest does not support sub-minute cron**, so a 30-second sweep was not schedulable as specified. | `FIXED` — `pg_cron` at 10 s behind an advisory lock, and the three conflicting latency numbers reconciled into one. |
| C4 | **Two clocks**: `serverNow` from the app process, `createdAt` from the database. With 4+ replicas, "clock skew injection — deadlines unaffected" **could not pass**. | `FIXED` — every deadline comparison is evaluated in SQL against the primary; `startedAt` comes from a `RETURNING` clause; a startup assertion checks replica clocks against the database. |
| C5 | The receipt did not commit to the answer keys (a mis-key was undetectable), a plain SHA-256 chain is self-attesting only against non-writers, and `canonicalJson` over `jsonb` is not well-defined. | `FIXED` — `keysHash` folded into `H₀`; final hash HMAC'd with a KMS-held key; the claim softened from "checkable rather than deniable" to what HMAC actually buys, and `jsonb` round-trip fidelity stated as a known limitation. |
| C6 | Concurrent submits were unspecified. | `FIXED` — a single conditional `UPDATE … WHERE status='IN_PROGRESS' RETURNING *`; zero rows means already submitted, so the stored receipt is returned. |
| C7 | `assessmentSpec` existed **twice** (a JSON column and the `AssessmentSlot` rows), and `AssessmentSlot.questionId` had no relation and no index. | `FIXED` — `AssessmentSlot` is the truth; the JSON column is gone; a real `question` relation added. |
| C8 | Client and server both "assigned" telemetry `seq`; and `serverTs @default(now())` is `transaction_timestamp()`, so a batch queued 10 minutes in IndexedDB had every `clientTs` clamped and the teacher's timeline was wrong. | `FIXED` — client sends `clientSeq`, server assigns `seq`, idempotency on `(attemptId, sessionId, clientSeq)` with `ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, plus an explicit `receivedAt`. |
| C9 | `seq = max+1` is a hot-row serialisation point with no stated primitive. | `FIXED` — dedicated `AttemptSeq` row with `UPDATE … RETURNING`. |
| C10 | Telemetry flush (25 events / 5 s = 300/min) **exceeded the 120/min limit**, so evidence was dropped exactly when a student was misbehaving fastest. | `FIXED` — limit raised to 600/min, 100-event client batches, and the `429` reports the dropped count so the timeline can show "evidence incomplete". |
| C11 | Answer-save limit of 60/min contradicted the plan's own stated principle that a student must never be throttled during an exam. | `FIXED` — 300/min, plus a client minimum-interval guarantee. |
| C12 | `isLate` existed only on the attempt, conflating two different meanings. | `FIXED` — `QuestionResponse.isLate` added; attempt-level and response-level meanings separated. |
| C13 | `extraTimePercent` on two entities with no precedence; the ⊕ in the policy resolution order was undefined. | `FIXED` — a documented fold with a named operator; mid-exam grants computed from remaining time. |
| C14 | `EXTRA_TIME_PERCENT` **rewrote `deadlineAt`**, breaking `INV-POLICY-1` and making `verify-receipt` report divergence on a legitimate action. | `FIXED` — `AttemptDeadlineExtension` rows; `effectiveDeadlineAt = deadlineAt + Σ extensions`; `deadlineAt` is never updated. |
| C15 | `BREAKS_ALLOWED` was unimplementable — no policy field, no `pausedAccumSec`, no pause term in the deadline. | `FIXED` — `pausedAccumSec` plus an audited pause/resume transition, included in the effective deadline. |
| C16 | "Student removed mid-exam → attempt frozen" had no state to freeze into, so held answers sat forever ungraded. | `FIXED` — `FROZEN` status handled by both the sweep and the grading pipeline. |
| C17 | A revoked exam session was a **dead end** — recovery via `start` returned `409 ATTEMPT_ALREADY_ACTIVE`. The most likely 3 a.m. support call. | `FIXED` — `POST /session/refresh` bound to the user session plus the attempt, with specified multi-tab UX. |
| C18 | The idempotent replay desynchronised the client's revision counter, returning a revision the row did not hold. | `FIXED` — idempotency is checked **first**; the original response bytes are stored and replayed. |
| C19 | `perQuestionTimeLimitSec` (policy) vs `Question.timeLimitSec` (item) had no precedence. | `FIXED` — `question.timeLimitSec ?? policy.perQuestionTimeLimitSec`, validated at publish. |
| C20 | `questionDeadlineAt` was never clamped to the attempt deadline, so a per-question countdown could show more time than the exam had. | `FIXED` — `LEAST(openedAt + limit, effectiveDeadlineAt + grace)`. |
| C21 | `PM` partial credit is gameable: on a 3-key/4-option item, selecting **all four** scores 2/3. And `07` §3 justified NG with a claim about "NG-free scoring" that the source does not contain. | `FIXED` — see §C (psychometrics) below; the whole taxonomy was rewritten. |
| C22 | `08` said "apply the published bias correction" without naming it, while its own property test asserted corrected D is never greater than uncorrected — which Kelley's factor violates. | `FIXED` — see §C. |
| C23 | Facility was uncorrected for guessing, while the interpretation bands assume a corrected p. | `FIXED` — `pFull`/`pCredit` split with bands defined per purpose. |
| C24 | Single-linkage chaining at a 5-gram threshold manufactures giant clusters. | `FIXED` — complete-linkage with a cluster-diameter report. |
| C25 | `INV-AUTH-1`'s "takes effect on the next request" was **false** given a 60-second Redis session cache. | `FIXED` — cache key deleted in the same transaction as every revoke/suspend/role change; exam path reads `revokedAt` from the primary. |
| C26 | Read-replica routing was unspecified, and the wrong answer breaks `INV-RELEASE-2`. | `FIXED` — a stated rule plus a lint rule: any query touching `releasedAt`, `finalScore`, `autoScore`, `manualScore` or `percentage` reads the primary. |
| C27 | `policySnapshot`/`rngSeed`/`variantMap` were non-null, but the attempt is created `NOT_STARTED` and the snapshot is taken at first start. | `FIXED` — nullable, with the write as `UPDATE … WHERE "policySnapshot" IS NULL` so two concurrent starts cannot both write it. |

## D. Psychometric review — the findings that changed the design

This review found the most serious problem of the three, and it was not a bug: **a citation had been edited to support the opposite of what it says.**

| ID | Finding | Sev | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P-1** | **`RN-05` had been edited.** It recorded the METRON finding as *"all partial-credit functions improve reliability over no-credit (NC) and no-guessing (NG)"*, and `07` §3 then cited it to justify **defaulting to NG** — NG *is* negative crediting, and "NG-free scoring" is not a defined term anywhere. `ADR-0020`'s context had quietly dropped the words "and no-guessing". A citation had been doctored to support the opposite conclusion. | **Critical** | `FIXED` — `RN-05` restored to what the source says. The NG default is replaced. **This is the finding that justifies re-checking every other citation in `21`, and it is now a standing review item.** |
| **P-2** | **NG is gameable exactly where the plan claimed it was not.** Under `correct − incorrect`, selecting all `M` options scores `2C − M`, positive whenever `C > M/2`. A 5-option item with a 3-option key — ordinary for "select all that apply" — pays a third of the marks for selecting everything. | Critical | `FIXED` — a publish-time rule: NG is only offered when `2C − M ≤ 0`. The `testGrader` harness displays the select-all score for every method. |
| **P-3** | **NG cannot coexist with the `0 ≤ points ≤ maxPoints` invariant the plan property-tests.** Clamping at zero is nonlinear and silently converts NG into "no penalty" for every student who guessed, destroying the guessing suppression NG exists to provide — and invalidating the point-biserial the plan reports. | Critical | `FIXED` — the floor is applied in the **attempt total only**, item statistics use the raw scale, and this is stated. |
| **P-4** | **SU's stated properties were wrong.** The plan said SU is "lenient — irrelevant selections are forgiven". Under subset matching, selecting an incorrect option makes the response *not* a subset and scores **zero**. SU is lenient about **omissions** and absolute about **commission**. A materially different mental model, and the copy taught the wrong one. | High | `FIXED` — corrected, and the copy now names the distinction. |
| **P-5** | **The taxonomy omitted the field's most-used method**: 1PM / partial all-or-nothing and simple proportional scoring. A "named taxonomy, not a boolean" that omits the default is incomplete. | High | `FIXED` — taxonomy is now **NC, 1PM, SU, RI, PM, PROPORTIONAL**, with **1PM as the default** (bounded, never negative, no guessing incentive, standard). |
| **P-6** | **The D bias correction was unnamed**, and the two distinct corrections in the literature point in **opposite directions**: Kelley's `2D/(1+D)` corrects restricted range and *increases* D (and returns −2.0 at D=−0.5, breaking the `[−1,1]` bound the plan asserts). The self-inclusion bias the plan actually describes is a *part-whole* problem whose remedy is computing the split from the **rest-score**, not a multiplicative factor. The plan's stated rationale and its stated fix were inconsistent. | Critical | `FIXED` — the correction is now named and **separated from the rest-score**: D is computed on rest-scores, the residual direction is stated (rest-score D is attenuated, uncorrected D is inflated), no multiplicative factor is applied at classroom `k`, and "print the item count next to D" is removed because the item count *is* the bias parameter. |
| **P-7** | **Correlation thresholds were an order of magnitude too permissive.** At `N = 30` a point-biserial of 0.30 has a 95% CI of **[−0.07, +0.60]** — statistically indistinguishable from zero. A teacher acting on it is acting on noise. The plan contains **no confidence interval anywhere**. | High | `FIXED` — `r_pb` requires `N ≥ 100` and is always shown as `r [95% CI]`. An item is flagged only when the **upper** bound is below the threshold. |
| **P-8** | **The multiple-comparison response did the opposite of what it should.** "Sort by severity and show the top issues" is selection on the dependent variable — it guarantees the teacher edits whichever items produced the largest random deviation. | High | `FIXED` — all items shown with intervals, never ranked by a single index, framed as a prompt for a human re-read rather than a defect list. |
| **P-9** | **"Facility" was two different numbers in one UI.** On an NG- or SU-graded item a class that identifies half the correct options scores `p ≈ 0.00` and lands in the `< 0.30` band, which the plan tells the teacher is "often a wording or key problem". The item is fine; the plan would manufacture a false revision. | High | `FIXED` — `pFull` and `pCredit` are separate fields with separate bands, keyed to purpose (formative / low-stakes / summative). |
| **P-10** | **Distractor analysis was not a statistic.** "The facility of students who selected it" does not parse; facility is a property of an item. The intended quantity is the **distractor discrimination index** `d_j = P(correct ∣ did NOT select j) − P(correct ∣ DID select j)`. The `> 40%` threshold is uninterpretable, and at the stated `N = 5` floor, 40% of five students is two. | High | `FIXED` — `d_j` implemented with an interval, compared against a null derived from the item's own facility, `N ≥ 30` for any flag, stratified by scoring method. |
| **P-11** | **The LID rule could not work at the product's own sample sizes.** A fixed `0.3` threshold flags **186 of 780 pairs by chance at N = 30**, and only 4.9 at N = 200. There was no N floor for LID at all, so 12-student quizzes would show residual correlations. The `AND topic` condition was also **inverted** — dependency *within* a declared cluster is expected and harmless; the concern is dependency *between* clusters. | High | `FIXED` — multiplicity-controlled threshold `tanh(z_{1−α/m}/√(N−3))`, floor 0.20, `N ≥ 100`; the *lower* threshold applies to pairs **outside** a declared cluster and no flag applies inside it. |
| **P-12** | **Cronbach's α was reported for the one use it cannot serve.** α is a function of the assembled fixed form; a deeper pool does not raise it, and computing it over pooled draws silently mixes a random-forms model into a fixed-forms statistic, so a single α is **unidentified** for a student who received one arbitrary draw. **Spearman–Brown was absent from the entire plan** — the standard tool for the question "what if the test were k items?", which is the plan's stated use. | High | `FIXED` — α restricted to fixed-form and labelled as such; **Spearman–Brown added for pool depth**; and the real quantity introduced: a **Monte-Carlo form-variability report** (draw 1,000 forms, report the SD of form means), because that SD *is* the form-equating error. |
| **P-13** | **"Dichotomised and therefore conservative" is wrong.** Dichotomising attenuates the correlation, but the result estimates a *different construct* (full-credit attainment), not a conservative version of the score the student actually receives. | Medium | `FIXED` — the polytomous item-total correlation (Pearson, both continuous) is computed and reported as the index of the score actually awarded. |
| **P-14** | **The expected-overlap table's verdicts were not a function of its own metric.** Expected *absolute* overlap `N²/M` grows with N while proportional overlap falls, so the column was anti-correlated with the metric. The plan's own example is decisive: M=12, N=3, C=30 gives an expected **1.98 colliding pairs** and an **86%** chance that two students get an identical form. Separately the warning `expectedCohortSize / targetOverlapTolerance` **cannot be implemented** — neither field exists in any file or in the schema. | High | `FIXED` — the UI shows `N/M`, **P(≥1 shared item)**, **P(identical pair)**, and expected distinct items used by a cohort of size C. `expectedCohortSize` added to `QuestionPool`; the undefined tolerance removed; `06` §2.2, `01` §7 and the schema now agree on one field name. |
| **P-15** | **The balanced draw strategies targeted the pool's composition instead of the blueprint's** — reproducing whatever skew the bank happens to have, which is exactly the failure the blueprint exists to prevent. And "within 1 item of balance" is **unfalsifiable** for realistic N: 3 items cannot be balanced across 6 demand levels. | High | `FIXED` — both strategies draw against **blueprint-declared quotas**; a balanced strategy requires `N ≥ 2 × strata` or falls back to a documented first-wins list; the "within 1 item" property is deleted. |
| **P-16** | **The invertibility test did not test invertibility, and missed the property that defeats collusion.** `grade(params, answer) === maxPoints` passes for a grader that returns full marks whenever the response equals the stored key. The real security property is **tolerance-band disjointness**: if two instances' tolerance bands overlap, a student who saw instance *i* submits its answer for instance *j* and is marked correct, and the anti-collusion premise evaporates silently. | High | `FIXED` — three properties: `grade` is total and bounded over adversarial responses; params → answer is injective; and **tolerance bands of distinct instances are disjoint**. |
| **P-17** | **Bloom's taxonomy is the wrong axis and was used as if validated.** Bloom is a *hierarchy* used as an unordered categorical axis; `cognitiveDemand` is a self-applied label by community authors with **no inter-rater reliability anywhere**, yet the blueprint and draw machinery depend on it; and it conflates the knowledge and process dimensions. One cell can be satisfied by a 1-mark identify item and a 20-mark problem, both tagged `apply`. | High | `FIXED` — `topic`/KC is the required tag, `cognitiveDemand` is optional and display-only, and **response process** (recognition / recall / production, derived from the question type rather than authored) is the second blueprint axis. A two-rater agreement study is a **release gate** before any draw strategy may depend on the label. |
| **P-18** | **Worst-case blueprint coverage by sampling can only over-report.** A minimum over sampled draws is one-sided in the wrong direction, so the tool would systematically understate how often a blueprint can fail. The exit criterion "reports worst case honestly" is unachievable by sampling. | High | `FIXED` — computed **exactly** as a bipartite b-matching feasibility question (max-flow / Hall's condition), which is instantaneous at these sizes. `BLOCKS_ALL_DRAWS` is now a true guarantee. |
| **P-19** | **RN-01's score-drop finding was misread, and the misreading was load-bearing.** "Scores fell 10–20% … interpreted as evidence that cheating had previously been common — the score drop is the intended effect." That inference is not available: the same drop is equally consistent with measurement variation, a different cohort, or the cognitive load of the controls — and the plan **cites that very finding** (Alessio: proctored students "scored significantly lower") in the same table, then adopts the reading that makes proctoring look effective. Worse, it gives the product a **target score reduction**. | **Critical** | `FIXED` — restated as a **confound, not a success metric**. A pre/post comparison cannot separate "less cheating" from "students performed worse under load". Measured and disclosed as an unexplained change requiring investigation, never as a target. |
| **P-20** | **"High specificity" was cited as reassurance; the quantity that matters — PPV — was never mentioned.** A hypothetical detector at 99% specificity and 30% sensitivity on a cohort with 10% cheating yields **PPV ≈ 25%**: three in four flags would be innocent. At 2% prevalence, PPV ≈ 6%. The plan reached the right conclusion by an argument that omits the number establishing it. | High | `FIXED` — the base-rate calculation is now the lead argument in `09` §1 and in the student-facing policy. It is arithmetic, not citation. |
| **P-21** | The 48-hour review period claimed in `RN-03` was attributed to a system the plan rejects, and was not implemented. | `FIXED` (K4) |

## E. Psychometric review — validity threats

| ID | Threat | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **V-1** | **Form inequivalence — the largest single threat in the product, never mentioned.** Students get different items by design; `01` §10.1 then reports one `percentage` per student from the resolved variant. Two students with identical knowledge who draw forms of different mean difficulty receive different percentages, and **nothing detects, bounds, corrects or discloses this**. The plan defers IRT (the tool) while shipping unbalanced random draws (the cause). The result is a number that is partly a measurement of the random seed. | `FIXED` — form-variability reporting added as a first-class analysis (Monte-Carlo SD of form means), the resolved variant shown on every gradebook row, the limitation stated in the plan and in the teacher-facing copy, and a v2 route (anchors + equating) recorded rather than pretended away. |
| **V-2** | **Pooled draws invalidate the item-total statistics the plan reports.** A student who draws hard items does badly on all of them, so the item's covariance with the total contains a between-form component that is not discrimination. Every index in `08` §2 is computed under a fixed-form model the plan does not satisfy. | `FIXED` — stated as a model caveat in the plan, surfaced in the UI, and **form-stratified statistics** reported where the cohort is large enough. |
| **V-3** | **LID becomes a *random* error under pooling, not just an inflation.** Which pair a student draws determines whether their score is inflated, producing non-exchangeable measurement error. A student who draws the dependent pair is advantaged by luck. There was no rule that a dependent pair must not both be drawable into the same form. | `FIXED` — a pool-level **compatibility constraint** so detected dependent pairs cannot co-occur in one form. |
| **V-4** | **Omitted and non-reached responses were treated as wrong.** No `isOmitted` field, and `answer` is non-null. On a 30-question timed exam the last items are blank because of **the clock, not ignorance**; those zeros depress facility and drive `r_pb` toward correlation with *speed*, and the plan would confidently misdiagnose a timing artefact as an item defect. | `FIXED` — `isOmitted` / `notReached` added to `QuestionResponse`; omission and non-reached responses are **excluded from item statistics** and counted separately; the plan states that the trailing items on a timed quiz are a mixture of knowledge, speed and carelessness. |
| **V-5** | **No careless-responding filter on timed exams.** The classic response-accuracy criterion (drop responses in the final seconds of a time-limited item) was absent. | `FIXED` — Hoving/Cattell/Fleishman response-accuracy filtering added as an option on timed assessments, with the filtered count disclosed. |
| **V-6** | **Omission and `needsHuman` were in no denominator.** A `needsHuman` response (a sim state that failed schema) is scored 0 or excluded, and the plan never says which — one branch penalises the class for a platform bug (which `INV-SIM-2` explicitly promises will not happen), the other computes on a non-random subset invisibly. | `FIXED` — every statistic displays its **denominator and its exclusion count**; `needsHuman` is excluded, never zeroed. |
| **V-7** | **Practice attempts were indistinguishable from real ones and consumed a graded attempt.** `allowPracticeAttempt` existed in the policy type but `ExamAttempt` had no `isPractice`, and `@@unique([assignmentId, studentId, attemptNumber])` against `maxAttempts @default(1)` meant a practice run **consumed the one real attempt** — making `maxAttempts: 1` exams impossible to practise, contradicting the very change `RN-04` claimed. | `FIXED` — `ExamAttempt.purpose ∈ {PRACTICE, GRADED}`; practice attempts do not count against `maxAttempts`, are excluded from every analytic query, and are visibly labelled to the student. |
| **V-8** | **The time-on-item conclusion was a validity error.** "90th percentile far above the estimate is either badly written or badly taught" ignores a third cause systematically present here: **a screen-reader or magnification user reading a maths or science item**, which reliably inflates the 90th percentile for a perfectly good item. | `FIXED` — the report flags **presentation** review as a distinct cause. This is now the one documented carve-out from `D13`'s blanket ban on stratification by accessibility variables, and it is the a11y community's most useful single diagnostic. |
| **V-9** | **A `practiceCheck` in a public resource is a published answer key for any item also used summatively.** Feedback-per-item is the standard key-extraction attack; `INV-RELEASE-1/2` is defeated instantly by the public route. No "retired from summative use" concept existed. | `FIXED` — a `retiredFromSummativeUse` flag with a publish-time check, and a cross-object reference from `practiceCheck` to the bank `Question`. |
| **V-10** | **`POST /start` returned the whole paper at once**, so every stem was in the student's hands before the timer started — photographable, scannable, structurally indexed. `RN-03`'s condition (b) is "content that is not internet-searchable"; shipping the whole form in one payload is the design most likely to make it searchable. | `FIXED` — `start` returns the **first question only**, with per-question fetch on advance. A side benefit is a much smaller pre-release audit surface. |
| **V-11** | **Graded sim questions were a search problem, not a measurement.** `sim:answer` is overwritable indefinitely; `chemistry.stoichiometry-balance` is specified with "unlimited retries"; hiding the `sim:gradePreview` *frame* is not the same as suppressing the sim's own rendered "Correct ✓". The item then measures parameter-space search — and its facility will look **excellent**. The question the reviewer was asked — does the score depend on the path or only the endpoint? — had, as specified, the worst possible answer. | `FIXED` — a `scoringSurface` field is mandatory on any sim usable as a question: `ENDPOINT_ONLY` (only the final answer is read) or `PATH_SENSITIVE` (the grader consumes a bounded interaction trace). Within an attempt, in-frame correctness feedback is **suppressed** and the number of graded submissions per attempt is bounded. |
| **V-12** | **`TERMINATE` was an automated punitive action that changes a score** — irreversibly discarding every unwritten item — while `ADR-0017` and `INV-TELEMETRY-1` both state client events "can never change a score" and there is "no automated punitive action". A direct contradiction inside the plan. | `FIXED` — `TERMINATE` no longer discards answers. It freezes the attempt, submits **everything already written**, and requires a human `IntegrityVerdict` before any score is affected. The escalation ladder's automatic steps stop at freeze-and-notify. |
| **V-13** | **The `EXAM` profile's actual strictness was undefined.** Whether `WARN` is inert or brutal depends entirely on `thresholds`, and **no defaults were specified anywhere**. The plan's entire "default to WARN" position was unimplementable. | `FIXED` — a defaults table, chosen against `RN-03`'s finding that controls which add cognitive load change the score. High `WARN` thresholds by default. |
| **V-14** | **Similarity clustering had a devastating false-positive case the copy did not name**: a student using a translation tool, speech-to-text, or dictation with a motor impairment. Cluster membership on such a student is a disability-adjacent false accusation in a teacher-facing report. | `FIXED` — the copy names these innocent causes explicitly; dictation and speech-to-text are added to the accommodation list; and **cluster membership can never be a factor in `IntegrityVerdict`** without a human noting the accessibility context. |

## F. Psychometric review — unfairness to students

| ID | Finding | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| U-1 | **The rate limiter met the outbox.** A 60/min limit against a 30-item outbox flushing in order under backoff inside a 60-second grace meant a student who lost connectivity for 90 s in the last two minutes had their answers 429'd and then abandoned. The student *did* save them; the server refused them. | `FIXED` — exempt from the burst limiter near the deadline, limit raised, `Retry-After` surfaced honestly, and an E2E test: kill the network at T−90 s with 15 unsaved responses and assert every one is acknowledged. |

| U-6 | **An inferred disability status was written to the student's record and kept for years.** `preflight.a11y.screenReaderGuess` was persisted, visible in a teacher-facing integrity report, retained for the life of the classroom + 24 months, and invisible and uncorrectable to the student. | `FIXED` — the inference is **transient**; only `relaxationsApplied` is persisted. | U-7 | **A student who cannot reach the network at all had no path.** `INV-POLICY-2` forbids a window shorter than the total limit, so a student with 20 minutes of connectivity on a train could not be granted one that fits. ## G. Psychometric review — teacher sustainability

| ID | Finding | Disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **T-1** | **100% manual review is not required by the product's promise, and the plan conflated the two.** The promise is *atomic release* — no student sees a partial result. That requires whatever the teacher has **decided** to be released together. It does not require every free response to be individually graded by a human. Real examination systems mark a moderated **sample**. | `FIXED` — **moderated sampling** is now allowed: a release batch may consist of moderated-and-sampled manual marking plus auto-graded items, with sample size and moderation rule as assignment configuration. | **T-2** | **The grading workspace was optimised for the speed at which marking is least defensible** — quick-score, save-and-advance, "clear 200 without a mouse" is a triage rate, not a marking rate. Time-pressured marking degrades reliability and biases against weaker scripts. | `FIXED` — the keyboard flow stays; marking rate is instrumented and reported; quick-scored responses are recorded as such and sampled for moderation. | **T-3** | **No inter-rater reliability anywhere, for the component that is 100% of the product's work.** The plan has 100% branch coverage and hand-verified fixtures for the *auto-grader*, and nothing for the human grading that is the product's actual promise. | **T-4** | **"Every teacher can see the queue" prevents delegation and creates unowned work.** A TA, a co-teacher on another timetable, or a departmental moderator cannot be given the queue without full classroom access. | `FIXED` — a first-class `Reviewer` role scoped to grading only, not to roster or integrity evidence; a reassignment path; and an "unowned queue, oldest item N days" report to the owner. |
| **T-5** | **Nothing scaled the pre-exam side, which is where the volume actually is** — 300 students × 30 questions = 9,000 reviewable auto-grade cells, with no sampling and no prioritisation. | `FIXED` — the auto-grade review surface defaults to **flag-driven plus a random 5% audit sample**, with "review all" as an explicit teacher choice, and the coverage of the review is reported. |
| **T-6** | **No bulk, evidence-triggered extension.** `extendDeadlineForAll` was all-or-nothing; at 300 students one student's connectivity collapse becomes 300 individual audited extensions. | `FIXED` — a bulk extension operating on the evidence record (`NETWORK_LOST`, `SAVE_REJECTED_LATE`, `SIM_LOAD_FAILED`, `CLOCK_SKEW_DETECTED`), with the affected set previewed and one recorded reason. ## The standing lesson

Two of the three reviewers found that the plan's *most confident-sounding* content was its weakest. The sandbox checklist was pre-ticked. The gate on 220 sims could not pass against its own catalogue. The release transaction could not meet its own SLO. A citation had been edited to support the opposite of what it says. The threat model was written after the system it reviewed.

The defence, now written into `plans/19` §9: **a gate that cannot fail is not a gate, and a citation that has not been checked against its source is not evidence.** Every finding above is recorded with a disposition rather than quietly fixed, so the next reviewer can see what was argued and what was decided.
